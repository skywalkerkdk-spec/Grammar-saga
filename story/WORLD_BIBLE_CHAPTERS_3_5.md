# Grammar Saga — World Bible and Chapters III–V
Status: proposed canon and campaign outline; not implemented. Chapters I–II existing data and IDs take precedence.

## The five arts
Structure (Period Crystal): sentences have shape. Grammar (Grammatica): relationships and time can be expressed. Vocabulary (Word Runes): things and ideas can be named. Understanding (Ancient Scrolls): context gives words meaning. Communication (Echoes): people exchange meaning. None alone can control minds; the antagonist attempts to combine corrupted copies into a coercive magical network. Ordinary Korean/Common Tongue remains unaffected.

## History
- Age of Bridges: five independent schools shared their discoveries; no single ruler owned language.
- Age of Division: a failed peace conference convinced a faction that disagreement itself caused suffering. The faction split from the schools and became the demon army.
- Age of Seals: the schools separated their artifacts for safekeeping. Lexion preserves records; Nova guards forbidden books. Neither is omniscient.
- Present: Fragment breaks the Period Crystal; Silentius corrupts Grammatica pages and manipulates their recovery. Both serve a higher command, provisionally called the Regent of Silence, whose identity is not yet public. Silentius is not the ultimate demon king. His defeat breaks the local spell, not the entire army.

## Chapter III — The Forgotten Words (잊힌 단어들)
Setting: Lexica Archipelago, islands linked by ferries. Names fade from magical signs; residents still recognize objects and speak Common Tongue. Villain Lexivor, the Collector, monopolizes magical names. Six proposed regions, each with two distinct quests:
| Region | Main incident | Side story | Learning focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rootshore | Harbor signs lose their names; compare cargo labels with original manifests | A gardener teaches a child plant names through family recipes | roots and word families |
| Prefix Peaks | A false warning reverses the meaning of a safe trail | Two siblings reconcile over a misunderstood note | prefixes and opposites |
| Suffix Forge | Apprentices lose their occupational titles; restore the guild register | A retired smith mentors a reluctant apprentice | suffixes and parts of speech |
| Twinmarket | A merchant sells misleading labels; compare words in context | Rival shopkeepers exchange recipes | synonyms, antonyms, collocations |
| Idiom Grove | Literal magic turns figurative expressions into illusions | A performer learns why a joke failed | idioms and figurative meaning |
| Namewell | Lexivor hoards the runes in a public naming ceremony | Islanders create a shared dictionary | vocabulary in context |
Act I: Modi notices a rune on a salvaged crate; sailor Byron introduces the islands. Act II: a forged dictionary links the Collector to Broken Quill. Act III: Cinder's verified testimony may unlock an optional correspondence scene; otherwise local witnesses explain the plot. Finale: the runes return to community archives rather than the party's private ownership.
Dialogue: Lexivor: "If I own every name, I own everything." / 모든 이름을 소유하면 모든 것을 소유하는 것이다.
Modi: "A name helps us find something. It does not make it yours." / 이름은 무언가를 찾도록 도와줘. 그것을 네 것으로 만들지는 않아.
Hook: two recovered scrolls describe the same voyage differently.

## Chapter IV — The Ancient Scrolls (고대의 두루마리)
Setting: the continent of Narratia. The Archivist of Ash, Censor, removes context from records to turn neighbors against each other. Six regions:
| Region | Main incident | Side story | Learning focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lantern Quay | A rescue report omits the rescued sailors' names | A child reconstructs a family journey | main idea and details |
| Chronicle Vale | Two diaries give different dates for a treaty | A baker reconstructs a lost recipe | sequence and chronology |
| Causeway | A damaged report falsely blames a village for a flood | Engineers compare two explanations of a broken mill | cause and effect |
| Mirror City | A public notice quotes only half a speech | Rival artists explain their intentions | inference and speaker intent |
| Evidence Reach | A forged historical claim divides two schools | Students interview elders about a local tradition | evidence and source comparison |
| Scroll Citadel | Censor offers one official version of history | The archives open to public annotations | synthesis and summary |
Act I: Lexion sends copies, not the original Grammatica. Act II: optional return of Palim's records exposes a forged chronology; mandatory local records suffice. Act III: the party discovers the Regent's network depends on readers accepting incomplete messages. Finale: multiple verified accounts are preserved, with uncertainty clearly marked.
Dialogue: Censor: "One story is easier to remember." / 하나의 이야기가 기억하기는 더 쉽지.
Ob: "But leaving out the truth changes the story." / 하지만 진실을 빼면 이야기가 달라져.
Hook: an ancient recording contains a voice asking for help from beyond the sealed sea.

## Chapter V — The Echoes of Eternity (영원의 메아리)
Setting: Resonantia, a federation of cities connected by magical voice towers. The Regent attempts to make the towers repeat one command. Six regions:
| Region | Main incident | Side story | Learning focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whisper Bay | Distorted ferry announcements strand travelers | A family reunites through a clear message | listening for details |
| Chorus Town | A choir loses the ability to take turns | A shy performer joins a duet | turn-taking and requests |
| Relay Plains | Messages arrive with changed intentions | Couriers resolve a mistaken invitation | clarification and paraphrase |
| Accord Bridge | Two delegations misunderstand each other's offers | Former rivals plan a shared meal | negotiation and polite disagreement |
| Echo Heights | Towers repeat a command instead of answering questions | A student interviews a retired tower keeper | questions and responses |
| Crown of Voices | The Regent activates corrupted copies of all five arts | Optional reunion with earlier allies | integrated comprehension and communication |
Finale: each companion contributes their established role; the player restores independent channels rather than imposing a new universal command. Earlier optional quests add acknowledgements, never required power or gates. Listening must have captions/replay; speaking must have accessible text alternatives and no mandatory microphone.
Dialogue: Regent: "Why should a world have so many voices?" / 어째서 세계에는 그렇게 많은 목소리가 있어야 하지?
Verbella: "Because every person has something to say." / 모든 사람에게는 하고 싶은 말이 있으니까요.
Ending: the five artifacts remain in public stewardship; the party returns to Beville, where the first bread-sharing story may receive an optional callback. The player can revisit all regions.
